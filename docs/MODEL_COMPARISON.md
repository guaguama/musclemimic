# Skeletal model comparison: LocoMuJoCo vs MuscleMimic

*Provenance of the two model families, and a dimensional / actuator audit of `OSLFullBodyRobot`
against LocoMuJoCo's joint-actuated skeleton.*

Analysis date: 2026-08-27. All numbers below are measured from compiled `MjModel` objects, not read
from XML text. Method and reproduction commands are in §8.

---

## 1. Answer in brief

**Provenance — no, they are not from the same source.** Two independent OpenSim conversions, by
different groups, from *different* source models:

- **LocoMuJoCo's `SkeletonTorque` / `SkeletonMuscle`** were hand-converted at TU Darmstadt from
  **Hamner, Seth & Delp (2010)**. The XML says so in its own header, and the model is fitted to one
  specific subject (`GZ_DW_sub01`).
- **MuscleMimic's `MyoFullBody` / `MyoBimanualArm`** are assembled from MyoSuite `myo_sim`
  components, which were auto-generated by **MyoConverter** from **Rajagopal et al. (2016)**.

There is exactly **one** point of contact between the two families, and it is not the one you might
expect: LocoMuJoCo's **`MyoSkeleton` is downloaded from `github.com/myolab/myo_model`** — MyoLab Inc.,
under a non-commercial research licence. So LocoMuJoCo *does* consume a MyoLab model, but it is a
different repository and a different model from the `myo_sim` components MuscleMimic uses, and it is
**position-actuated, not muscle-actuated**.

The two OpenSim source models are cousins rather than strangers — both descend from Delp's gait model
lineage — but they diverged well before either MuJoCo conversion, and the divergence is measurable
(§5).

**Is `OSLFullBodyRobot` on par?** Yes, and on the axes that matter it is better grounded:

| Axis | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Overall scale | Comparable. Leg length within 5%, hip height within 1.5%, total mass within 5% |
| Segment proportions | **Ours better.** Matches Dempster/Winter segment masses to within 0.2 pp; LM's thigh is +2.4 pp heavy and its femur:tibia ratio is 0.76 (human ≈ 1.0) |
| Actuator scheme | **Ours more capable.** Normalised position servos with principled Kp/Kd; LM uses open-loop torque motors |
| Torque limits | **Ours grounded, LM's are not.** Ours are measured from the sibling muscle model (agree to ~1%); LM's torque gears disagree with LM's *own* muscle model by 0.28×–10.5× |
| Joint ranges | **Ours tighter and more anatomical.** LM allows ±120° hip rotation and ±90° subtalar |

§7 records the findings acted on: the analyzer's contaminated inertia probe (§7.1, resolved) and the left/right torque asymmetry (§7.2).

## 2. Provenance in detail

### 2.1 LocoMuJoCo's skeleton — Hamner 2010, hand-converted

The first four lines of `skeleton_torque.xml` are unambiguous:

```xml
<?xml version="1.0" encoding="utf-8"?>
<!--Created by Guoping Zhao, Lauflabor, TU Darmstadt.-->
<!--Based on Hamner2010 OpenSim model.-->
<!--No muscles.-->
<!--Simplify knee joint to a single DoF revolution joint.-->
<!--This model parameters (e.g. contact detection etc.) haven't been optimized.-->
<mujoco model="GZ_DW_sub01">
```

Points to note:

- **Guoping Zhao** is the second author of the LocoMuJoCo paper, and runs the Lauflabor Locomotion
  Laboratory at TU Darmstadt. This is a first-party conversion, not a third-party import.
- **Hamner 2010** = *Muscle contributions to propulsion and support during running*, J. Biomech.
  43(14) — reference [10] in the LocoMuJoCo paper, which says only "The skeleton models are converted
  models from [10]". The XML confirms it.
- **`GZ_DW_sub01`** — a subject-specific fit, not a generic 50th-percentile model. This matters for
  §3.
- The joint naming is pure OpenSim gait-model convention: `hip_flexion_r`, `knee_angle_r`,
  `ankle_angle_r`, `subtalar_angle_r`, `mtp_angle_r`, `lumbar_extension`, `arm_flex_r`, `arm_add_r`,
  `arm_rot_r`, `elbow_flex_r`, `pro_sup_r`, `wrist_flex_r`, `wrist_dev_r`.
- **The header disclaims its own tuning**: "This model parameters ... haven't been optimized." §5
  shows exactly where that bites.

`skeleton_muscle.xml` is the same skeleton with 92 muscles added on the legs and trunk. The arms stay
torque-actuated — 14 motors — which the class docstring states plainly:

> This Humanoid consists of 92 muscles on the lower limb. The upper body is torque actuated.

Both are scalable via `BaseSkeleton.scale_body()`, and the scaling laws are the ones the paper
describes:

```python
body.pos          *= s        # lengths     linear
body.mass         *= s ** 3   # masses      cubic
body.fullinertia  *= s ** 5   # inertias    quintic
actuator.gear     *= s ** 2   # torques     quadratic   (muscles: actuator.force *= s**2)
```

with an assertion that off-diagonal inertia terms are zero, so the quintic scaling stays valid. The
paper's four age presets (infant ≈ 2 y, child ≈ 5 y, teenager ≈ 12 y, adult) are just four values of
`s`. **MuscleMimic has no equivalent** — `MyoFullBody` is a single fixed 84.3 kg adult, and subject
fitting is done instead by SMPL shape-fitting at retarget time.

### 2.2 LocoMuJoCo's MyoSkeleton — MyoLab, downloaded

`loco_mujoco/utils/myomodel_init.py` clones a pinned commit and requires interactive licence
acceptance:

```python
fetch_git(repo_url="https://github.com/myolab/myo_model.git",
          commit_hash="619b1a876113e91a302b9baeaad6c2341e12ac81",
          clone_directory="myo_model", clone_path=PATH_TO_CUSTOM_MODELS)
```

> A permissive license for non-commercial scientific research of the MyoSkeleton by the **MyoLab Inc.**
> is available.

This is the shared-lineage point. But `MyoSkeleton` is **not** muscle-actuated: `myoskeleton.py`
`_add_actuators()` adds one generic actuator per joint ("Adds a generic actuator to each joint"), and
the class docstring describes "one position controller per joint" over 151 joints. So LocoMuJoCo's
only MyoLab-derived model is a *position-controlled* skeleton — useful as a high-DoF kinematic
target, not as a muscle model.

### 2.3 MuscleMimic's models — Rajagopal 2016, auto-converted

`myo_sim/leg/README.md` is explicit:

> The myoLeg mujoco musculoskeletal (MSK) models are generated with taking
> [Rajagopal's full body gait model](https://github.com/opensim-org/opensim-models/tree/master/Models/RajagopalModel)
> as close reference.

and describes a three-stage automatic pipeline — element conversion (bone meshes, joint definitions,
muscle paths, wrapping objects), then **moment-arm optimisation**, then **muscle force–length
optimisation** — followed by documented manual corrections (wrapping objects removed for glutes and
psoas to stop path jumping; `lmin` raised from 0.1 to 0.05 on four muscles to kill negative forces).
Contact geometry is fitted to the **Yeadon** measurement method.

`myo_sim/leg/myolegs.xml` header: `Copyright 2020 Vikash Kumar, Vittorio Caggiano, Huawei Wang`.

Component inventory (from `myo_sim/README.md`), and how `MyoFullBody`'s 416 muscles decompose:

| Component | DoF | Muscles |
|---|---:|---:|
| MyoLeg (both legs) | 20 | 80 |
| MyoBack | 18 | 210 |
| MyoArm (×2) | 27 each | 63 each = 126 |
| **Total** | | **416** ✓ |

The OSL prosthesis comes from `myo_sim/osl/`, authored by Chun Kwang Tan, Elizabeth Wilson and Adan
Yusseff Dominguez Ruiz on top of the MyoSuite team's work. **Our OSL actuators are carried through
verbatim** — byte-identical to upstream:

```xml
<!-- myo_sim/osl/assets/myolegs_osl_assets.xml : 508-509
     and models/myoLeg80_OSL_KA/assets/myolegs_assets_OSL_KA.xml : 510-511  -->
<motor name="osl_knee_torque_actuator"  gear="49.4" ctrlrange="-2.88 2.88" joint="osl_knee_angle_r"/>
<motor name="osl_ankle_torque_actuator" gear="58.4" ctrlrange="-2.88 2.88" joint="osl_ankle_angle_r"/>
```

Peak moments: knee 49.4 × 2.88 = **142.3 N·m**, ankle 58.4 × 2.88 = **168.2 N·m**.

Reading notes:

- **LM is near 1:1 actuator-to-joint** (31 motors / 32 joints) because its knee was deliberately
  simplified to a single revolute. **Ours is 29 actuators / 78 joints** because Rajagopal's knee is a
  6-DoF assembly (`knee_angle_beta_rotation1`, `knee_angle_translation1/2`, `knee_angle_rotation2/3`)
  driven by equality constraints — hence 44 constraints vs LM's 8.
- `nq` drops 124 → 84 between our muscle and robot models: exactly the **40 finger joints** removed by
  `disable_fingers`. Body count is unchanged because the bodies remain, welded.
- Our robot is **4.2 kg lighter** than LM, and 1.8 kg lighter than intact `MyoFullBody` — the
  transfemoral amputation replaces distal right-leg tissue with a lighter prosthesis.

## 4. Dimensions and mass distribution

### 4.1 Segment lengths (left side, intact in all three models)

Measured from joint anchor positions after `mj_forward` at `qpos0`, and **independently verified**
from body-frame offsets (`body_pos`) — the two methods agree to <0.1 mm.

| Segment | LM SkeletonTorque | MyoFullBody | **OSLFullBodyRobot** | ours vs LM |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Femur (hip → knee) | 36.6 cm | 40.4 cm | **40.4 cm** | +10.6% |
| Shank (knee → ankle) | 48.0 cm | 40.0 cm | **40.0 cm** | −16.7% |
| **Hip → ankle (whole leg)** | **84.6 cm** | **80.4 cm** | **80.4 cm** | **−4.9%** |
| Rearfoot (ankle → mtp) | 14.5 cm | 13.8 cm | **13.8 cm** | −5.1% |
| Pelvis width (hip ↔ hip) | 17.1 cm | 15.5 cm | **15.5 cm** | −9.4% |
| Upper arm (shoulder → elbow) | 33.7 cm | 29.1 cm | **29.1 cm** | −13.7% |
| Forearm (elbow → wrist) | 28.0 cm | 25.8 cm | **25.8 cm** | −7.7% |
| Hip joint height at `qpos0` | 90.8 cm | 92.1 cm | **92.1 cm** | +1.5% |

**Whole-leg length agrees within 5% and hip height within 1.5%** — the two models are the same size
of person. LM is a slightly larger build overall (86.6 kg, longer arms).

**The femur:tibia split is where they genuinely disagree**, and it is LM that is anomalous:

| | femur | tibia | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| LM SkeletonTorque | 36.55 cm | 48.02 cm | **0.76** |
| OSLFullBodyRobot | 40.44 cm | 40.01 cm | **1.01** |
| Human (typical) | — | — | ≈ 0.95–1.10 |

A femur 24% shorter than the tibia is outside normal human proportion. I verified this two independent
ways, so it is a real property of the model, not a measurement artifact — most likely a non-uniform
artifact of the `sub01` subject fit, possibly compounded by LM's knee-centre redefinition when the
knee was collapsed to one DoF. **Ours is the anatomically sound one.** Practical consequence: if you
ever want to compare swing-leg dynamics or knee-moment arms against LM's skeleton, the segment
inertias will not correspond even though total leg length does.

## 5. Actuator comparison

### 5.1 Two different schemes

**LocoMuJoCo — open-loop torque motors.** `gaintype=fixed`, `gainprm=[1,0,0]`, `biasprm=[0,0,0]`,
`ctrlrange=[-1,1]`, `gear` = peak torque. No position feedback, no damping term; joint damping lives
in `dof_damping` (default `damping=1.0`, `armature=0.01`, `stiffness=1.0`).

**Ours — normalised position servos.** Written as `<general biastype="affine">` with

```
gainprm[0] = Kp * half_range
biasprm    = (Kp * midpoint, -Kp, -Kd)
ctrlrange  = [-1, 1]
```

so that

```
force = Kp * (midpoint + half_range * ctrl - q) - Kd * qdot
```

i.e. `ctrl = ±1` lands **exactly** on the joint limits. That makes `DefaultControl`'s [-1,1]
normalisation an identity map — a nice property, and worth knowing before anyone "fixes" the apparent
mismatch between `gainprm[0]` and `-biasprm[1]`, which is intentional (their ratio is `half_range`).

Three sizing rules, all motion-independent:

1. `forcerange` = peak moment the **sibling muscle model** can produce about that coordinate.
2. `Kp = min(forcerange / 0.35 rad, I_eff * 150²)` — saturates at 0.35 rad of error, bandwidth-capped
   at ωₙ = 150 rad/s.
3. `Kd = 2·ζ·√(Kp·I_eff)` with ζ = 1.

`I_eff` in rules 2 and 3 is the equality-constrained inertia of the **mechanism** at the reconciled
`qpos0`, measured with the position servos disarmed *and contacts disabled*. Both matter — see §7.1
for why measuring it on the servo'd model understated the arms by 48–62%, and why leaving `qpos0`'s
21.47 mm arm–torso interpenetration in place tripled shoulder inertia.

Rule 3 is a stability requirement, not a preference: `Kd·dt/I = 2·ζ·ω·dt` must stay below 2 for
explicit Euler, and `mjDSBL_EULERDAMP` — enabled by the OSL environments — makes `dof_damping`
implicit but **does not cover an actuator's `biasprm[2]`**. The measured stability numbers at the
sizing pose are all 0.03–0.30, comfortably inside the limit; the worst is 0.600 at the 2 ms timestep
the training env actually uses.

### 5.2 The grounding test

LocoMuJoCo ships a muscle **and** a torque version of the *same* skeleton, which lets me apply the
identical measurement to both sides — an unusually clean test. For each actuated coordinate I computed
the isometric moment ceiling (all agonists at `act=1`, antagonists at 0, summing positive and negative
moment columns separately, swept over the joint's range).

**LocoMuJoCo: its torque limits do not match its own muscle model.**

| Joint | LM muscle ceiling | LM torque `gear` | gear / muscle |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `mtp_angle` | 4.8 N·m | 50 | **10.5× over** |
| `hip_rotation` | 96.5 | 600 | **6.2× over** |
| `knee_angle` | 178.5 | 600 | **3.4× over** |
| `hip_adduction` | 267.5 | 530 | 2.0× over |
| `ankle_angle` | 313.4 | 500 | 1.6× over |
| `hip_flexion` | 331.0 | 275 | 0.83× |
| `lumbar_extension` | 217.5 | 160 | 0.74× |
| `lumbar_bending` | 242.0 | 160 | 0.66× |
| `subtalar_angle` | 180.5 | 50 | **0.28× under** |
| every arm joint | **0.0** | 250 / 50 | **no muscle sibling at all** |

The arm row is the striking one: LM's muscle model has **zero arm muscles**, so its 250 N·m shoulder
and elbow gears and 50 N·m wrist gears are not derived from anything. And 250 N·m at the elbow is
roughly 3× adult elbow-flexion MVC; 250 N·m at `pro_sup` (forearm pronation) is ~17× physiological.
These are placeholder round numbers, exactly as the XML header warns.

**Ours: sized from the muscle sibling by construction, and it holds.** From
`analyze_osl_fullbody_robot_actuators.py`, `flim` (in XML) vs freshly measured muscle capacity:

| Joint | XML `flim` | measured muscle | Joint | XML `flim` | measured muscle |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| `flex_extension` | 305 | 307 | `hip_flexion_l` | 560 | 561 |
| `lat_bending` | 430 | 431 | `hip_adduction_l` | 420 | 418 |
| `axial_rotation` | 270 | 271 | `hip_rotation_l` | 185 | 186 |
| `elv_angle_r` | 155 | 153 | `knee_angle_l` | 545 | 546 |
| `elbow_flex_r` | 85 | 84 | `ankle_angle_l` | 425 | 424 |
| `shoulder_rot_r` | 90 | 90 | `subtalar_angle_l` | 135 | 134 |

Agreement is exact on all 27 joints once the rounding rule is applied: the XML stores
`flim = max(5.0, round(peak / 5) * 5)`, while the diagnostic prints the raw measurement beside it — so
`pro_sup` 15 vs 17.4 and `mtp_angle_l` 5 vs 6.5 are the rounding, not a discrepancy (§7.1).

### 5.4 Why the underlying muscle capacities differ

The trunk and knee differences are real model differences, not measurement noise:

| | LM SkeletonMuscle | MyoFullBody | OSLFullBody |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| muscle actuators | 92 | 416 | 390 |
| Σ F_max | 71.3 kN | 142.0 kN | 107.6 kN |
| mean F_max | 775 N | 341 N | 276 N |
| max F_max | 3549 N | 5322 N | 5322 N |

At the left knee specifically — **the same 13 muscles cross it in both models**, so this is a pure
strength comparison:

| | crossing muscles | Σ F_max | mean \|moment arm\| |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| LM SkeletonMuscle | 13 | 11.89 kN | 2.58 cm |
| Ours | 13 | **20.81 kN** | 1.72 cm |

**1.75× the muscle force at the knee.** This is the expected Rajagopal-vs-Hamner difference: Rajagopal
2016 set muscle strengths from MRI-measured muscle volumes, producing a materially stronger model than
the gait2392/Hamner lineage. The consequence is visible at the extremes:

- LM's knee-extension ceiling is **178.5 N·m** — *below* typical adult knee-extensor MVC (~250–300
  N·m). A known limitation of that lineage.
- Ours is **545 N·m** — an all-agonist theoretical ceiling well above MVC, so there is headroom.

Both are theoretical ceilings (every agonist at full activation, zero velocity), not voluntary maxima,
so neither should be read as a predicted human torque. But the *relative* statement is sound: our
muscle model can generate knee extension moment that LM's cannot, and our servo limits inherit that.

## 7. Findings acted on

### 7.1 RESOLVED — the analyzer's `I_eff` probe was contaminated; gains re-derived contact-free

**Status: fixed.** `constrained_inertia` now measures on a servo-neutralised, contact-free copy, the
probe is guarded for linearity, and `POSITION_SERVOS` has been re-derived. 15 of 27 rows changed.
An earlier revision of this document claimed the XML had drifted from the generator on four joints;
that was wrong. A later revision claimed the shipped table was correct and should be frozen; that was
half right — the table was self-consistent, but its arm gains were sized against an artifact.

**What was wrong.** `constrained_inertia` was called on the *already-actuated* robot. Since
`biasprm[0] = Kp · midpoint`, at `ctrl = 0` every servo commands its joint-range midpoint rather than
holding position. At `qpos0` that saturates 7 of 29 actuators (`shoulder_elv` demands 493.8 N·m
against ±110; `knee_angle_l` 1630.6 against ±545) and drives `|qacc|` to 7070 rad/s².

**The mechanism — corrected twice.** Earlier revisions blamed force saturation and marginal joint
limits. Both are wrong:

- **Not saturation.** An affine-bias servo's force depends only on `(q, q̇)`. Both probes share the
  same `q` with `q̇ = 0`, so the force — clipped or not — is identical and cancels exactly in the
  difference. With contacts disabled, servos-live and servos-free give the *same* `I_eff` (1.1227).
- **Not joint limits.** Disabling them moves `shoulder_elv_r` by 2.9e-13. Only one
  `mjCNSTR_LIMIT_JOINT` row is ever active.
- **Not ground contact.** At `qpos0` the root is at z = 1.0 with no floor contact at all.

The actual cause is **arm–torso self-contact**. At `qpos0` both humeri sit **21.47 mm inside**
`thorax_coll1` at ~1346 N per side, via explicit `<pair>` elements. Under the servo loads a pyramidal
friction facet multiplier clamps to zero across the probe (2.1999 → 0.5935 → 0) — a piecewise-linear
transition. `nefc` stays 53 throughout, so **a constant constraint-row count is not a constant
inequality active set**; that is what made the artifact look deterministic and solver-independent
(bit-identical across 100→5000 iterations, tolerance 1e-8→0, and Newton/CG/PGS).

Measured damage, servos live vs servo-free:

| Joint | servo-free `I_eff` | servos live | error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `shoulder_elv_r` | 3.37448 | 1.73829 | −48% |
| `shoulder_elv_l` | 3.37422 | 1.26971 | −62% |
| `shoulder_rot_l/r` | 0.02138 | 0.01019 / 0.01036 | −52% |
| `elbow_flex_l/r` | 0.05150 | 0.04659 / 0.04720 | −9% |
| legs and torso | — | agree to 4–5 s.f. | negligible |

It also manufactured a 27% left/right asymmetry at a bilaterally symmetric pose, which is how the
problem surfaced.

**Why the old table was not simply restored.** Neutralising the servos alone reproduced the committed
values exactly — but that basis still includes the 21.47 mm interpenetration, which *triples* measured
shoulder inertia (3.37448 vs 1.12269). ζ = 1.00 held only there: contact-free it was 1.73
(`shoulder_elv`) and 1.99 (`shoulder_rot`), and at all-midrange `shoulder_rot` was 0.72. Decisively,
on the contact-free inertia the old `shoulder_rot` gains gave `Kd·dt/I = 1.735` at the 2 ms training
timestep — **87% of the Euler bound of 2**, not the 0.600 previously documented. So the probe now also
sets `mjDSBL_CONTACT`, and the arms are sized against the free mechanism.

**What changed in the table** (all 27 `forcerange` values are unchanged — the capacity path compiles
the muscle model separately and was never affected):

| Joint | Kp | Kd |
|---|---|---|
| `shoulder_elv_r/l` | 314.3 | 65.13 → **37.57** |
| `shoulder_rot_r/l` | 257.1 → **121.6** (now ω-capped) | 4.69 → **1.62** |
| `elbow_flex_r/l` | 242.9 | 7.07 → 6.20 |
| `pro_sup_r/l` | 42.9 | 0.78 → 0.64 |
| `lat_bending` | 1228.6 | 90.33 → 87.52 |
| `flexion_r/l` | 82.1 → 81.0 | 1.09 → 1.08 |
| `hip_adduction_r` / `_l` | 671.4 / 1200.0 | 23.23 → 23.15 / 39.63 → 39.37 |
| `axial_rotation`, `hip_rotation_r` | unchanged | 30.26 → 30.21, 10.62 → 10.61 |

The torso and hip shifts are second-order: the arm–torso contact was bracing those coordinates too.
Result: ζ = **1.0000** on all 27, worst `Kd·dt/I` at 2 ms = **0.600** (`shoulder_rot_r`). The arms are
now materially softer — `shoulder_rot` Kp halved — which is the correct consequence of not sizing them
against a 1346 N interpenetration. A 6 s fixed-root hold shows arm excursion of 2–5 × 10⁻³ degrees
peak-to-peak with monotonic settling, so no ringing was introduced.

**Two traps recorded for anyone re-deriving this.**

1. `geom_contype[:] = 0` and `geom_conaffinity[:] = 0` do **not** disable these contacts — explicit
   `<pair>` elements are still evaluated and `nefc` stays 53. Only `disableflags |= mjDSBL_CONTACT`
   works.
2. `qpos0` is only an XML default and never a pose the model holds (training initialises from
   trajectory frames), but its arm–torso interpenetration corrupts **any** measurement taken there.
   Account for it in future sizing or diagnostic work.

Guardrails now in place: the probe raises `RuntimeError` on a nonlinear response
(`|Δa(2) − 2Δa(1)| / |2Δa(1)| > 1e-3`) instead of returning a plausible wrong number, and rejects
non-finite or non-positive responses rather than clamping them to a huge inertia.
`test_servo_gains_satisfy_the_sizing_rules_at_the_reference_pose` recomputes ζ and Kp from a freshly
measured `I_eff` — verified to fail on all four contaminated arm joints — so a bad regeneration can no
longer pass by agreeing with itself. The trajectory diagnostic path warns instead: 6 of 21 frames are
still nonlinear even contact-free, from joints riding their limits.

### 7.2 The left/right torque asymmetry is correct — confirm it is what you want

Our right-side hip limits are about half the left:

| | left | right | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| hip flexion | 560 | 285 | 0.51 |
| hip adduction | 420 | 235 | 0.56 |
| hip rotation | 185 | 160 | 0.86 |

This is not a sizing bug. Transfemoral amputation removes the muscles that insert distal to the section
— the hamstrings and rectus femoris both cross the hip and insert below the knee — so the residual
limb's hip flexion/extension capacity genuinely drops by roughly half, which mirrors reduced hip
strength in transfemoral amputees. Because `forcerange` is measured from the muscle sibling, the servo
inherits that asymmetry automatically.

That is exactly right **if** the robot model is meant as a torque-actuated ablation of the same body —
same dynamics, actuation swapped. It would be wrong if you intended a symmetric "reference robot"
whose two legs are equally capable. Worth being deliberate about, because it will show up as
asymmetric gait authority during training.

## 8. Method and reproduction

LocoMuJoCo's model assets were moved to a separate `loco_mujoco_models` package on 2026-03-03
(`3edea01`, "outsourced the models to separate repo") and that package is not installed here. The
XMLs and meshes were recovered from git history instead — no network access needed:

```bash
UP=~/src/00_AMBER/00_projbackflip/loco-mujoco
SP=/tmp/lm_models && mkdir -p $SP
git -C $UP archive '3edea01^' loco_mujoco/models | tar -x -C $SP --strip-components=2
head -6 $SP/skeleton/skeleton_torque.xml          # provenance header

# MyoSkeleton provenance
sed -n '/fetch_myoskeleton/,/Successfully/p' $UP/loco_mujoco/utils/myomodel_init.py

# MuscleMimic-side provenance
sed -n '1,12p' ~/src/00_AMBER/01_projpegleg/myo_sim/leg/README.md
sed -n '1,10p' ~/src/00_AMBER/01_projpegleg/myo_sim/leg/myolegs.xml
grep -n 'osl_.*torque_actuator' ~/src/00_AMBER/01_projpegleg/myo_sim/osl/assets/myolegs_osl_assets.xml

# scaling laws
sed -n '/def scale_body/,/return mjspec/p' $UP/loco_mujoco/environments/humanoids/base_skeleton.py

# our actuator diagnostic (read-only; ~2 min)
.venv/bin/python scripts/analyze_osl_fullbody_robot_actuators.py
```

All structural numbers come from compiling each model with `mujoco.MjModel.from_xml_path` and reading
`body_mass`, `body_subtreemass`, `body_pos`, `jnt_range`, `dof_armature/damping`,
`actuator_gainprm/biasprm/gear/ctrlrange/forcerange`. Segment lengths were computed both from
`data.xanchor` after `mj_forward` and from raw `body_pos` offsets; the two agree to <0.1 mm.

Isometric moment ceilings use the fact that MuJoCo muscle force is linear in `act` with no
inter-muscle coupling, so the maximum moment about a coordinate is the linear-program optimum "every
agonist at `act=1`, every antagonist at 0" — obtained by summing positive and negative moment
contributions separately, swept over each joint's range. For LM I applied no transmission-ratio
correction, which is valid here: its only joint equality constraints are on `subtalar`, `mtp` and the
wrist coordinates, and none of the joints compared in §5.2 drives a follower. MuscleMimic's script
does apply the correction, which its docstring notes matters by 1.65–1.9× for the trunk.

Caveats worth carrying forward:

- Ceilings are isometric (`qvel = 0`), so the force–velocity factor sits at its optimum. They are
  upper bounds, not predicted voluntary torques.
- Heights are measured at `qpos0`, and neither model is verified to be exactly floor-contacting there,
  so **hip height and segment lengths are the robust dimensions**; absolute ground clearance is not.
- Dempster/Winter fractions are population means; a ±1 pp deviation is unremarkable, which is why only
  LM's +2.37 pp thigh is called out.
